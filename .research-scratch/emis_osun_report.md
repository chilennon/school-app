## 2. Core thesis
EMIS significantly predicted effective planning and administration in Osun State public primary schools, mainly via personnel management, data utilization and resource adequacy (p.1, p.7). SUBEB and LGEAs used EMIS data for promotion, transfer, material distribution and policy implementation (p.4–5). Yet it failed at school level: headteachers lacked the confidence and skills to update it, so administrators did not embrace EMIS (p.1, p.5).

## 3. Method
- Descriptive survey (p.3). Population: headteachers and teachers in Osun State public primary schools; size **not stated**.
- Sample: 190 respondents — 50 headteachers + 140 teachers — from 10 public primary schools in the 3 senatorial districts (p.3).
- Multistage: one LGA randomly per senatorial district (3 LGAs); proportionate random selection of 10 schools; all headteachers of sampled schools purposively included, teachers randomly sampled (p.3).
- Instrument: researcher-designed EEMISPAPSQ questionnaire, expert-validated; test-retest reliability 0.75 and 0.89 (subscales not stated) (p.4).
- Analysis: frequency counts/percentages for research questions; multiple regression for the hypothesis, α = 0.05. Response rate and collection dates not stated (p.4).

## 4. Key quantitative findings
Tables 1–3: n = 190; Table 4: n = 50 headteachers.
- Personnel management: material distribution easier via EMIS 65.8%; EMIS consulted for teacher transfers 60.5%; timely promotion from EMIS records 84.2% (p.4).
- Data utilization: officers collaborate with teachers/heads on data gathering 78.9%; SUBEB staff consult EMIS data 71.1%; planning department carried along 84.7% (p.4–5).
- Resource adequacy: computers/storage adequate 77.9%; EMIS departments in all LGEAs 89.5%; facilities/materials adequate 82.6%; equipped officers 78.9% (p.5).
- User-friendliness (heads): can update data at school 40.0% (20/50); action plans run without school visits 46.0%; SUBEB dissemination effective 50.0%; training easy/friendly 46.0% (p.5).
- **H₀ (no significant joint influence) — REJECTED.** R = .836, R² = .699, adjusted R² = .693, SE = 0.361; ANOVA F(4,185) = 119.54, p < .001; 69.9% of variance explained (p.6).
- Coefficients, all p < .05: personnel management β = .365; data utilization β = .318; resource adequacy β = .247; user-friendliness β = .174 (p.7).

## 5. Documented EMIS / school-data failure modes
- Staff capacity: headteachers lacked the confidence/skills to update EMIS; only 40% could update at school, 46% found training easy (p.1, p.5).
- No usable school-level access point; updating happens away from schools; EMIS "not user-friendly" at school level (p.5–6, p.8).
- Weak outward information flow: only 50% rated SUBEB dissemination effective (p.5); administrators did not adopt EMIS despite state-level embrace (p.1).
- Lit-cited only, not measured: insufficient ICT, resource inadequacy, weak administrator skills (p.1–2); lack of ICT/trained personnel (p.2); integration and data-quality weaknesses (p.3); resource-allocation difficulty and insufficient training (p.3).
- Data-quality metrics, timeliness, coverage gaps, funding levels and data-use audits: **not stated**.

## 6. What the paper recommends
- SUBEB should strengthen continuous training of headteachers on using EMIS (p.8).
- Government should invest in maintaining and upgrading ICT facilities across all LGEAs (p.8).
- Make EMIS user-friendly with accessibility at school level (p.8).
- Continued user capacity-building for sustainable planning and administration (p.1, p.8).
- No costed plan, budget figure, data standard or NEMIS/interoperability ask is given.

## 7. Product implications — all items are my inference
1. Make the **headteacher the primary data-entry user**, not SUBEB — 40% at-school update is the key signal (p.5).
2. **Offline-first sync as core architecture**, since school-level access is the stated gap.
3. Design for **low digital confidence**; teach by doing for the 54% who found training hard (p.5).
4. Prioritise **teacher records, promotion, transfer and material distribution** (60.5%–84.2% agreement) (p.4).
5. Ship **one-tap exports for SUBEB/LGEA planners**, matching the 84.7% planning-unit link (p.4–5).
6. Push information **outbound** (SMS/offline inbox) against the 50% dissemination score (p.5).
7. Encode the **LGA → school hierarchy** and SUBEB/LGEA roles (p.3, p.5).
8. **Validate at entry with role-based permissions**, given lit-review data-quality concerns (p.3).
9. **Hardware is not the binding constraint** — 77.9%–89.5% reported adequate devices and units (p.5).
10. Give headteachers **their own dashboard**; user-friendliness was weakest but significant (β = .174) (p.7).
