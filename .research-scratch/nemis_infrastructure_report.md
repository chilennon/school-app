# Report — Adequacy of Infrastructural Facilities for NEMIS (Anambra State)

## 1. Citation & topic
- **Title:** "Adequacy of Infrastructural Facilities Required for the Implementation of Nigeria Education Management Information System Policy in Anambra State."
- **Authors:** Carol O. Ezeugbor (PhD) & Kingsley K. Obiekwe
- **Institution/country:** Nnamdi Azikiwe University, Awka, Anambra State, Nigeria
- **Year:** not stated in extracted text (Vol. 14, No. 2; one source record dated 2017)
- **Study type:** descriptive survey, census of NEMIS nodes
- **Journal:** Journal of the Nigerian Academy of Education, Vol. 14, No. 2, pp. 12–20

## 2. Core thesis
NEMIS policy (2007) and its 2009 guidelines decentralised EMIS, requiring states to set up, staff and equip the system, with specified infrastructure at every state and LGA node. Auditing Anambra State against the FME (2009) specification, the authors find provision grossly inadequate — 5 of 9 items (55.6%) at the state node and no item fully available across the 21 LGA nodes — blaming low funding and missing dedicated NEMIS offices. Without that infrastructure, reliable planning data cannot be produced.

## 3. Method
- **Design:** descriptive survey (p.4), via Fraenkel, Wallen & Hyun (2012).
- **Population/sample:** 22 nodes — 1 at the state Ministry of Education, 21 at the LGAs; whole population used (census, no sampling) (p.4).
- **Location:** Anambra State, Nigeria — Ministry of Education, Awka, and 21 LGA headquarters (p.4–5).
- **Instruments:** two observation checklists, 9 items each, one per node level, completed by direct researcher observation (p.4–5).
- **Analysis:** frequency and percentage against FME (2009) specs (p.5).
- **Research questions:** 2 — LGA-node adequacy; state-node adequacy (p.4).

## 4. Key quantitative findings
- Of 9 required facilities, only 5 (55.6%) available at state level (p.1, p.7).
- No facility 100% available at LGA level (p.7); abstract says "none was available" (p.1) — inconsistent with Table 1.
- **LGA nodes (n=21):** servers 0/21 (0%); computers 8/126 (6.35%); printers 13/42 (30.95%); photocopiers 1/21 (4.74% as printed); laptops, scanners, internet/V-Sat, 24-hour UPS and external backup all 0/21 (0%) (p.5–6).
- **State node (n=1):** server 1/1, computers 10/10, laptops 3/3, photocopier 1/1, internet/V-Sat 1/1 (100%); printers 2/3 (66.67%); scanners 1/2 (50%); UPS 0/1 and external backup 0/1 (0%) (p.6–7).

## 5. Documented EMIS / school-data failure modes
- EMIS "could not perform optimally; data were either not available or unreliable" (p.2).
- "Disorganized" management: different data held for the same indicators — enrolment, teacher numbers (p.2).
- Agencies/partners collected "different and discordant sets of data on similar indicators"; decisions were not evidence-based; indicators could not reach the UNESCO Institute of Statistics (p.2).
- Causes (Rufai, 2010): inadequate funding, weak capacity, poor infrastructure, skewed incentives (p.2).
- **Power:** no 24-hour UPS at any node (0/21 LGA, 0/1 state), no external archiving anywhere (p.6–7).
- **Connectivity:** no internet/V-Sat at any LGA node; only the state node had it (p.5–6).
- **Hardware:** 8 computers for 21 LGAs against 126 expected; no servers, laptops or scanners at LGA level (p.5–6).
- **Facilities:** no dedicated LGA nodes — "embedded within other offices and departments" (p.7).
- **Funding:** low state funding (p.7); officials allegedly divert money meant for educational facilities (p.7).
- **Staffing:** states must staff and equip EMIS (p.3); staffing adequacy not measured — not stated.
- **Data use:** whether data were analysed, published or used — not stated.

## 6. What the paper recommends
1. Budget NEMIS implementation cost in the annual budgets of the concerned departments, ministries and agencies (p.8).
2. Partner with non-governmental and international development partners for funding and infrastructure (p.8).
3. Set up an effective monitoring mechanism for implementation (p.8).
4. Isolate local-level NEMIS nodes from other departments for efficiency (p.8).
5. Proper funding, supervision and monitoring by the state government (p.8).

## 7. Product implications (offline-first, mobile-first, Nigeria)
1. Assume **zero connectivity at LGA/school level** (0/21 LGA nodes online, p.5): offline-first with opportunistic sync. (inference)
2. **Energy-first:** no 24-hour UPS anywhere (p.6–7) — crash-safe writes, resume after power loss. (inference)
3. **No server dependency** (0 LGA servers, p.5): phone or single PC is the node of record. (inference)
4. **Durable local backup as core** — external archival backup absent everywhere (p.6–7): encrypted store, one-tap SD/USB export, later off-site sync. (inference)
5. **Shared-device multi-user sessions:** 8 computers for 21 LGAs (p.5) — role switching with attribution. (inference)
6. **Paper-free workflows:** printers scarce (30.95% at LGA), scanners absent (0/21) (p.5–6). (inference)
7. **One canonical data dictionary** for enrolment/teacher indicators to end "discordant" agency data (p.2). (grounded; implementation is inference)
8. **Partitioned workspace for embedded nodes** (p.7) rather than an assumed dedicated EMIS office. (inference)
9. **Resumable, low-bandwidth submissions** to the connected state tier (p.6). (inference)
10. **Validation, completeness flags and review queues** for the documented "unreliable" data problem (p.2). (inference)

## 8. Caveats
- Stated limitations: none.
- Abstract says "none was available" at LGA level (p.1); Table 1 shows items present and p.7 says none was "100% available" — inconsistency.
- FME (2009) lists a *mini* server for LGA nodes (p.4) but the checklist item reads "mid-range server" (p.5).
- Table 2's header says "at each local government node" though it reports the state node (p.6).
- Arithmetic: 1/21 printed as 4.74% (exact 4.76%) (p.5); p.7 prints 66.6% where the table gives 66.67%.
- Extraction noise: truncated "Two (NISN) were used" (p.1); unreadable checklist titles (p.4); dangling "A infrastructural specifications…" (p.5); scrambled research questions (p.4); "descriptive esurvey design" (p.4); garbled UNESCO URL (p.9).
- Citation mismatches: text Humbert and Chad (1998) vs reference 1995; text "Shahi, 2012" vs reference "Shashi (2012)".
- Comparison studies cited concern UBE/physical education, not EMIS; the fund-diversion claim (p.7) is unsourced.
