# Analysis: Adisa (2026) — EMIS and record keeping in Nigerian secondary schools

## 1. Citation & topic
- **Title:** "Educational Management Information System: Its Relevance to Recording Keeping and Processing in Secondary Schools"
- **Author:** Olusumbo Adisa (sole), Dept. of Educational Management, University of Ibadan, Nigeria
- **Journal:** UEJTL: Universal Education Journal of Teaching and Learning (E-ISSN 3047-8235), 2026, 3(2), 162–170; DOI 10.63081/uejtl.v3i2.140; Original Research; CC BY-SA
- **Type:** descriptive survey (quantitative) on EMIS relevance to record keeping in public secondary schools, Badagry LGA, Lagos State

## 2. Core thesis
EMIS is a database plus ICT applications for collecting, storing, retrieving and analysing educational data so managers can plan on validated, timely information. Here it plays only a minimal role in decisions. The bottleneck is infrastructural and motivational, not demographic or procedural: gender, department and cabinet-vs-computer-lab storage make no measurable difference. Digitising storage is not information management.

## 3. Method
- Descriptive survey; population = students in public secondary schools, Badagry LGA, Lagos (semi-urban case typical of national infrastructure challenges)
- Sample: 100 students, 5 schools, 20 each, stratified random sampling
- Instrument: two-section questionnaire; four-point Likert items (SA/A/D/SD)
- Reliability: split-half odd/even correlation 0.87; analysis: means plus Chi-Square at 0.05
- H01 gender vs managerial skills; H02 department vs record keeping; H03 lab vs cabinet storage vs processing

## 4. Key quantitative findings
- H01 gender: χ²=7.817, df=1, p=0.452; not significant (p.5)
- H02 department: χ²=11.207, df=9, p=0.262; not significant (p.5)
- H03 storage location: χ²=5.347, df=8, p=0.720; not significant (p.5)
- All three null hypotheses accepted at 5% (p.5)
- Sample 100 students, 20 per school; reliability 0.87 (p.5)
- World Bank funded 40+ EMIS-component projects "some years back" (p.3)
- No other outcome statistics (e.g. % schools with working computers) stated: none

## 5. Documented EMIS / school-data failure modes
- EMIS plays a "minimal role" at Federal, State and Local Government Educational District levels; planning starts with it yet planners "deviate from it" (p.1–2)
- Records mostly manual, kept by the head alongside other duties; retrieval "not easy" (p.2)
- Many nations "not improving on the survey done"; data objectives set by policy rather than need (p.3)
- Performance data largely informal, from project reports citing long setup, missing internal expertise, maintenance difficulty; little consensus on efficacy (p.3)
- Chronic underfunding; unreliable electricity, no hardware, no internet (p.4)
- No administrator technical expertise; resistance from staff used to manual systems; poor data discipline and maintenance culture; insufficient training; manual overload on heads (p.4)
- Government imprest "meager and delayed"; schools must raise community/alumni funds but may not levy learners (p.4)
- EMIS is meant to avoid duplication, fill information gaps and provide feedback, implying these persist (p.2–3)
- Lab computers broken, un-networked or intermittent; "technology as fetish" — equipment mistaken for use (p.6)
- No procedural manuals; unclear data-stewardship roles; a "weak organizational script" (p.6–7); web/cloud shift raises student privacy concerns (p.3)
- NEMIS is not stated; the national EMIS is discussed generically

## 6. What the paper recommends
- Make data management a centralised institutional function with defined stewardship roles and protocols
- Redesign workflows, retrieval protocols, entry standards and training with any technology; avoid hardware-centric fixes
- Mandate and fund basic ICT infrastructure; champion data discipline; practical, merit-based training
- Operational asks (p.7): communicate EMIS methodology and SWOT; set retention/disposal time-lags; design filing manuals; store records on computer/cloud; provide clean, air-conditioned storage; employ or train record officers
- Future research: larger multi-site samples, mixed methods, administrator interviews

## 7. Product implications (offline-first, mobile-first)
1. Don't sell digitised storage; lab vs cabinet changed nothing (p.5–6). Sell retrieval speed, standard entry and reporting (inference)
2. Treat no-power/no-network as default: offline capture, later sync, low battery cost, no networked-lab dependency (inference; p.4, p.6)
3. Fewest-taps entry and recurring registers for the overloaded head teacher (p.2, p.4) (inference)
4. Validate at entry: required fields, range checks, duplicate-student detection, audit trail (p.4, p.6) (inference)
5. Core objects: enrolment, attendance, grades/progression, scheduling, admission (p.3) (inference)
6. Phone-first capture; desktops are scarce, broken or un-networked (p.4, p.6) (inference)
7. Ship retention/disposal schedules and printable exports (p.7) (inference)
8. One-click aggregate government returns for upward reporting (p.2–3) (inference)
9. Record community/alumni income separately from learner levies (p.4) (inference)
10. Build in privacy, cloud backup and adoption support (printed procedures, named data steward) (p.3, p.4, p.7) (inference)

## 8. Caveats
**Stated (p.5, p.7):** the 100-student sample is not representative of all Nigerian secondary schools; one LGA only; findings indicative of Badagry; managerial skill judged from student perceptions, not administrative staff; future work needs larger multi-site samples and mixed methods.

**Additional weaknesses:** students answered while hypotheses concern administrators; null results fit low power (df 8–9 for H02/H03); the "suppression effect" reading is interpretive, untested.

**Likely extraction noise / artifacts:**
- Title says "Recording Keeping"; article history dates inconsistent (Received 29 01 2026, Revised 02 12 2026, Accepted 04 01 2026) (p.1)
- Recommendation numbering gap "(3) (4) The filling manual…", likely "filing"; item (3) missing
- "Educational System Information System" (p.3), likely a typo
- Reference mismatches: in-text "Hua & Herstein (2003)" vs "Haiyan, H., & Herstein, J." plus a duplicate "Hua, H. & Herstein, J."; "Thorn's (2001)" vs "Thorn, C. A."; "Hider et al. (2018)" mis-cites a rheumatology RCT
- Off-topic LIMS/ELN laboratory framing (p.4, p.6); split-half correlation mislabelled "Cronbach's Alpha" (p.5)
- Footer remnants; AI-use statement names ChatGPT/Google Scholar (p.8)
